# Project leads and admins may decide a challenge

Until now only the challenged memory's author could decide a challenge, until it had been open three days or the author had left the team. The author is a party to the dispute, and a stubborn one could sit on a correct challenge for those three days. A challenge against project memory may now also be decided, at any time, by a lead of the project that memory is in or by one of the team's admins (the owner is one); a challenge against team memory, by one of the team's admins. The author still decides, at any time, as before.

## Considered options

- **Only the lead decides.** Rejected: it makes memory hierarchical, hands every disagreement to someone who was in neither session, and slows the common case, where the author simply agrees, to one person's attention.
- **The author decides, and the challenger may appeal a rejection to the lead.** Rejected: a second step and a second state for a challenge, to reach what a lead deciding outright already gives.
- **Shorten the fallback period instead.** Rejected: it opens a stalled challenge sooner without changing who is trusted, so the interested party still has the first word alone.

## Consequences

- The challenged memory's project is the one that counts. When the challenging memory sits in another project, leading that project gives no standing.
- Team memory belongs to no project, so a project lead has no standing there: a decision about it reaches every project.
- A lead or an admin who raised the challenge can't decide it in that capacity, or they would be judge of their own request; another lead or an admin can. A lead who is the author decides as the author.
- The three-day fallback to any member, and any member deciding at once when the author has left, stay as they were.
- An author's memory can now be superseded or retired without their say. So the record explains itself: the audit log records in what capacity the decider decided (author, lead, admin, fallback, author left), and when it was not the author, the author's next briefing says who decided and in what capacity.
- Deciding stays governing, not writing: the console may do it (ADR-0009).
